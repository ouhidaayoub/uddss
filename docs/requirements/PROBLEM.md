# Problem

The Computer Science Department regularly receives requests for information.

Examples include:

* How many students graduated?
* How many students repeated a year?
* How has enrollment changed?
* What is the success rate?
* What are the department's academic indicators?

The people requesting this information are usually:

* Head of Department
* Deputy Head of Department
* Program / Specialty Heads
* Faculty Administration
* University Administration

The answers generally exist, but obtaining them is often difficult.

## Why Is It Difficult?

The required information is not located in a single place.

It may originate from:

* PROGRES
* Department databases
* Department spreadsheets
* Paper archives

To answer a single question, department staff may need to locate several sources, compare them, clean inconsistencies, combine information, and verify correctness.

## What Happens Today?

When information is requested, the process generally involves:

1. Finding the relevant data.
2. Accessing the available sources.
3. Cleaning and validating the data.
4. Combining information from multiple sources.
5. Verifying correctness.
6. Producing the final answer.

The most time-consuming activities are:

* Finding data.
* Cleaning data.
* Combining data from multiple sources.

## What Happens When Information Is Missing?

When the required information cannot be found immediately:

* Old files may be searched.
* Multiple sources may be compared.
* Knowledgeable individuals may be contacted.
* Information may be reconstructed manually.

This increases the time required to answer the request and introduces additional opportunities for inconsistency and error.

## Why Is This A Problem?

The current process:

* Requires significant manual effort.
* Consumes time.
* Depends heavily on human knowledge.
* Can produce mistakes.
* Can produce inconsistent results.

As a result:

* Reports may be incorrect.
* Department decisions may be incorrect.
* Planning activities may be affected.
* Confidence in the produced information may decrease.

Because important department activities depend on reliable information, delays and mistakes can negatively affect decision-making and operational effectiveness.

## What Do We Want Instead?

The department should be able to obtain trusted academic and administrative information quickly and consistently.

People should spend less time finding, cleaning, combining, and validating data, and more time understanding information and making decisions.

The same question should produce the same answer regardless of who prepares the information.

Reliable information should be available in a repeatable, efficient, and trustworthy manner.
